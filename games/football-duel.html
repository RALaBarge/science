How to play and the physics

Every play starts with a question. The defense picks a science topic card, and the offense answers a question from it before the 15-second play clock runs out. Answer right and the ball is snapped. Answer wrong and the quarterback is sacked (lose 5 yards and the down). Let the clock run out and it’s delay of game: back 5 yards, and you replay the down with a new question.

Throwing. After the snap, receivers run their routes and you click (or tap) the spot where you want the ball to come down. Lead your receiver: throw to where they will be. Pick the throw before you click: Bullet (20°) gets there fast but stays low, so a defender in the way can grab it. Lob (50°) sails over defenders but hangs in the air, so they have time to catch up. Touch (35°) is in between. Throw before the pass rusher gets to you.

Jump balls. If a receiver and a defender both get to the ball, it’s a jump ball: both players get the same question and the first right answer wins it. Offense wins: catch. Defense wins: interception. A wrong answer locks you out, so the other player can take their time. If a defender gets to the ball alone, the defense answers one question to hold on for the interception.

The physics. A thrown ball moves forward at a steady speed while gravity pulls it down, so it flies in a curve (a parabola). Its time in the air is 2 × speed × sin(angle) ÷ gravity. With no air, 45° throws farthest. Gravity here is real Earth gravity: 9.8 m/s², about 10.7 yards per second per second.

Get 10 yards in 4 downs for a new set of downs. A touchdown is 6 points, and one more right answer adds the extra point. Each team gets the same number of drives; most points wins.
