# Pick a trade: a catalog of hands-on careers (draft)

Status: draft for review. Built 2026-11-04 from official Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data, a Washtenaw Community College (WCC) program survey and union craft lists. Nothing here gives addresses; the point is to show what exists and how people get in.

## How to read this

- **Part 1** goes deep on 15 trades chosen for size (many Michigan jobs), a clear way in, steady or growing demand, and work that is hard to hand to software.
- **Part 2** gives every other occupation in the data one short line, grouped by family.
- **Part 3** lists trades the BLS data cannot count.
- **Appendix** is the complete table of all 306 occupations with the numbers.

**About the numbers.** Worker counts and median wages are BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), May 2025, wage-and-salary jobs only. Projected change and annual openings are BLS Employment Projections, 2025 to 2035, which include self-employed workers, so the two will not match for the same job. Michigan figures are blank when BLS does not publish them for small occupations. "AI exposure" and the one-line descriptions are judgments written by a language model without web access; they are not measurements and have not been checked line by line. "WCC" means Washtenaw Community College and is only filled in where the WCC survey could say.


# Part 1: Fifteen trades in depth

### Electricians (47-2111)
**The work.** Electricians install, repair, and maintain wiring, fixtures, controls, and power systems in homes, businesses, factories, and construction sites. The work includes reading plans, running conduit, connecting panels, testing circuits, and troubleshooting faults. Conditions can include tight spaces, heights, outdoor weather, and live or locked-out equipment. Safety rules and electrical codes shape most tasks.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like figuring out why a system is not working.
- Are careful with safety and detail.
- Can work with your hands in awkward spaces.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and an apprenticeship. Union apprenticeship: International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW); the report names IBEW Local 252 / Ann Arbor Electrical JATC. WCC offers credit only for electricians, not a standalone electrician program; the report names Apprentice Completion (CTAC) and Journeyman Industrial (APJPIM) for apprenticeship credit.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 23,530 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $76,270. US median wage: $63,190. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +9.2%; about 72,700 openings a year in the US. Worker counts are wage-and-salary jobs and projections include the self-employed, so the two will not match.
**AI and automation.** Software can help with load calculations, code checks, drawings, and diagnostics. Robots are less able to handle routing wire through existing buildings, bending conduit around obstacles, and troubleshooting unusual faults. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Electrical power-line installers and repairers; Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers; Solar photovoltaic installers; Industrial machinery mechanics.

### Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers (49-9021)
**The work.** HVACR mechanics and installers set up, repair, and maintain heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration systems in homes, stores, plants, and other buildings. Work includes testing controls, replacing parts, checking refrigerant charge, and troubleshooting comfort or cooling problems. Conditions can include rooftops, tight mechanical spaces, hot or cold areas, and handling refrigerants.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like mechanical and electrical troubleshooting.
- Can work in tight or high places.
- Are careful with refrigerant and safety rules.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and long-term on-the-job training. Union apprenticeship: United Association (UA), which covers HVACR service technicians; WCC provides classroom and credit for UA apprenticeships. WCC programs: HVAC and Refrigeration - Residential (CTHVRR), HVAC and Refrigeration - Commercial Trade (CVHVCT), and HVAC and Refrigeration (APHVCR). The residential certificate prepares for the third-class refrigeration licensure exam.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 12,590 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $60,850. US median wage: $61,010. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +10.9%; about 40,600 openings a year in the US. The numbers show steady demand for workers who can install and service climate-control equipment.
**AI and automation.** Software can help with diagnostics, controls, energy tuning, and fault codes. Physical work such as running line sets, replacing compressors, and working in tight spaces still needs people. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Electricians; Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters; Industrial machinery mechanics.

### Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters (47-2152)
**The work.** Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters install and repair pipe systems for water, gas, drainage, steam, and process fluids. Work includes measuring, cutting, threading, joining pipe, and testing systems for leaks. Settings range from homes to commercial buildings, plants, and construction sites, often in cramped or dirty conditions.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like hands-on problem solving.
- Can work in tight spaces and on call.
- Are careful with code and safety requirements.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and an apprenticeship. Union apprenticeship: United Association (UA); the report names Greater Michigan UA Local 190 JATC. WCC offers credit only for this trade, not a standalone plumbing program; Apprentice Completion (CTAC) and Journeyman Industrial (APJPIM) are the named credit pathways.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 14,120 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $80,190. US median wage: $63,800. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +6.8%; about 42,000 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to a well-paid Michigan trade with a solid number of openings.
**AI and automation.** Software can help with layout, code checking, and diagnostics, and prefabrication can reduce some on-site labor. Cutting, fitting, and repairing pipe in existing buildings are hard to automate fully. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers; Electricians.

### Industrial machinery mechanics (49-9041)
**The work.** Industrial machinery mechanics maintain, diagnose, and repair production equipment such as conveyors, robots, hydraulic and pneumatic systems, and machine tools. Work often happens in factories and plants, where mechanics may remove parts, align components, and test repairs. Conditions can include noise, grease, confined spaces, and scheduled shutdowns.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like taking machines apart and fixing them.
- Can use electrical, mechanical, and computer skills together.
- Are willing to learn many types of equipment.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. Union list: International Union of Operating Engineers (IUOE), which includes mechanics. The report also names a Michigan Regional Council of Carpenters and Millwrights apprenticeship for millwrights, who install and align industrial machinery. WCC programs: Industrial Electronics Technology (CFIET), Robotics Technician (CTROBT), Automation Specialist (CVAMSP), and Mechatronics - Robotics and Automated Systems (APMRAS).
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 17,310 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $64,250. US median wage: $64,520. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +17.8%; about 44,400 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to strong projected growth and many openings in manufacturing maintenance.
**AI and automation.** Software can monitor equipment, predict failures, and guide diagnostics. Robots can do some repetitive tasks, but repairing and installing machinery usually requires hands-on judgment. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Machinists; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers; Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers.

### Automotive service technicians and mechanics (49-3023)
**The work.** Automotive service technicians and mechanics inspect, diagnose, and repair cars, trucks, and other light vehicles. Work includes engines, brakes, steering, electrical systems, batteries, and computer-controlled components. Most work is in shops or dealerships, often with lifts, scan tools, greasy parts, and time targets.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like diagnosing problems with tools and computers.
- Can work around vehicles and small parts.
- Are willing to keep learning as vehicles change.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and short-term on-the-job training. The union lists do not name a specific apprenticeship for automotive service technicians. WCC programs: Automotive Services Technician (CTASVT), Advanced Automotive Services Technician (CVASV2), Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Safety and Fundamentals (CTEHSF), and Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Service Technician (CTEHST).
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 22,280 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $54,120. US median wage: $50,620. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +5%; about 66,200 openings a year in the US. The numbers show a large occupation with many openings, mostly to replace workers.
**AI and automation.** Scan tools and software can find faults, guide repairs, and calibrate driver-assist systems. Physical repair, disassembly, and judgment about worn parts still need technicians. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists; Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers; Industrial machinery mechanics.

### Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists (49-3031)
**The work.** Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists repair and maintain heavy vehicles, buses, fleets, and diesel engines. Work can include brakes, electrical systems, fuel systems, transmissions, and preventive maintenance inspections. The job is often in fleet garages or roadside settings, with dirty, noisy, and time-sensitive conditions.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like big engines and heavy-duty systems.
- Can follow safety rules around large vehicles.
- Are okay with on-road breakdowns or shift work.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. The union lists do not name a specific apprenticeship for this trade. WCC does not offer a standalone diesel/heavy truck program; Automotive Services Technician (CTASVT) includes diesel exposure.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 9,020 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $61,420. US median wage: $61,770. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +3.6%; about 24,400 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to steady work and ongoing openings to keep fleets moving.
**AI and automation.** Telematics and diagnostic software can flag problems before they become breakdowns. Hands-on repair of engines, brakes, and hydraulics is not easy to automate. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Automotive service technicians and mechanics; Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers; Industrial machinery mechanics.

### Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers (51-4121)
**The work.** Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers join and cut metal using heat, pressure, and filler materials. Work includes reading drawings, preparing metal, checking weld quality, and sometimes repairing parts in the field. Conditions can involve heat, sparks, confined spaces, and protective gear.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like steady, precise handwork.
- Can follow safety procedures closely.
- Are interested in metal fabrication or repair.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training. Union apprenticeship: United Association (UA), which lists welders among its trades. WCC programs: Welding and Fabrication Principles (CTWLDS), Welding and Fabrication Advanced Applications (CVWLDN), and Welding Technology (APWLDF). The report also notes a proposed Marine Structural Welding and Ship Repair certificate, but it is not yet in the catalog.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 13,330 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $49,990. US median wage: $53,750. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +2.4%; about 40,300 openings a year in the US. The numbers show slow growth but many openings because welding remains a replacement-heavy trade.
**AI and automation.** Robotic welding cells can handle repetitive production welds, and software can help with inspection and programming. Fit-up, field work, and unusual joints still rely on skilled welders. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Machinists; Industrial machinery mechanics; Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters.

### Machinists (51-4041)
**The work.** Machinists set up and operate machine tools to make or repair precision metal parts. Work includes reading blueprints, choosing cutting tools, measuring parts, and running manual or CNC machines. Most work is in manufacturing or machine shops, often with standing, machine noise, and careful safety rules.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like precision and measurement.
- Are comfortable with math and technical drawings.
- Can focus on repeatable quality.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. Union list: International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers (IAM), which covers machinists. WCC programs: Advanced Manufacturing (CNC) - Operation Technician (CTMTOT), Advanced Manufacturing (CNC) - Programming and Setup Technician (CTMTPS), and Advanced Manufacturing (CNC) - Machine Tool Setup, Operation and Programming (APMTOP).
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 20,350 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $52,360. US median wage: $58,750. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +1%; about 26,100 openings a year in the US. The numbers show little expected growth, but replacements will still create openings.
**AI and automation.** CAD/CAM software can generate tool paths, and automated cells can run long production jobs. Setup, tooling choices, and first-article inspection still need skilled machinists. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Industrial machinery mechanics; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers; Operating engineers and other construction equipment operators.

### Carpenters (47-2031)
**The work.** Carpenters build, install, and repair structures and fixtures made of wood and other materials. Work can include framing, concrete forms, roofing support, drywall backing, and finish carpentry. Jobs are often on construction sites or in homes, with outdoor weather, heights, lifting, and changing work locations.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like making physical things from plans.
- Can handle outdoor and rough work.
- Are careful with measurements and safety.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and an apprenticeship. Union apprenticeship: Michigan Regional Council of Carpenters and Millwrights. WCC has Construction Technology but no standalone carpentry program: Construction Technology I (CTCON1), Construction Technology II (CVCON2), and Construction Technology (APCONT); the latter programs prepare for the Michigan Builder's License exam. Kalamazoo Valley Community College has an articulation agreement with the carpenters/millwrights apprenticeship.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 18,590 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $61,680. US median wage: $60,580. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +3.9%; about 62,800 openings a year in the US. The numbers show a large trade with many openings, even with modest growth.
**AI and automation.** Software can help with layout, estimating, and prefabrication. Site conditions, material handling, and fitting work still require carpenters. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Operating engineers and other construction equipment operators; Electricians; Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters.

### Electrical power-line installers and repairers (49-9051)
**The work.** Electrical power-line installers and repairers build, maintain, and repair overhead and underground power lines. Work includes climbing poles or towers, stringing wire, replacing hardware, and responding to outages. The job is outdoors and can involve heights, storms, and emergency call-outs.
**Good fit if you...**
- Are comfortable working outdoors and at heights.
- Can follow strict electrical safety rules.
- Are willing to work emergency storms.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. Union apprenticeship: International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW), which covers electrical utility work. WCC offers nothing for this trade. Other Michigan routes named in the report: Lansing Community College lineworker pre-apprenticeship/certificate/degree, Mott Community College non-credit lineworker training with Consumers Energy, Alpena Community College, and the Joint Michigan Apprentice Program.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 2,800 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $106,890. US median wage: $95,320. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +10.3%; about 10,900 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to a smaller but well-paid trade with good projected growth.
**AI and automation.** Software, sensors, and drones can help inspect lines and locate faults. The physical work of climbing, handling cable, and repairing damaged lines is hard to automate. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Electricians; Wind turbine service technicians; Operating engineers and other construction equipment operators.

### Wind turbine service technicians (49-9081)
**The work.** Wind turbine service technicians inspect, troubleshoot, and repair wind turbines and their electrical, hydraulic, and mechanical systems. Work often includes climbing tall towers, replacing components, and doing scheduled maintenance. Conditions include height, weather, rural wind farms, and possible travel to sites.
**Good fit if you...**
- Are comfortable climbing and working at height.
- Like electrical and mechanical troubleshooting.
- Can work in remote or travel-based jobs.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and long-term on-the-job training. WCC offers nothing for this trade. The union lists do not name a wind-specific apprenticeship. Other routes named in the report: Kalamazoo Valley Community College Wind Turbine Technician Academy and Universal Technical Institute; the report says the status of these options should be verified.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 70 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $62,800. US median wage: $64,120. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +29.5%; about 1,500 openings a year in the US. The numbers show very fast projected growth, but Michigan's current base is small and total openings are limited.
**AI and automation.** Monitoring software and drones can help predict failures and inspect blades or components. Climbing towers and repairing gear in tight nacelles still requires technicians. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Solar photovoltaic installers; Electricians; Industrial machinery mechanics.

### Solar photovoltaic installers (47-2231)
**The work.** Solar photovoltaic installers assemble, mount, and connect solar panel systems on roofs, ground mounts, and other structures. Work includes measuring layouts, attaching racking, running wiring, and helping test the finished system. The job is often outdoors and can involve heights, lifting, and hot or sunny conditions.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like outdoor construction work.
- Are careful with heights and electrical basics.
- Can work as part of a crew.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training. WCC offers nothing for solar or renewables. The union lists do not name a solar-specific apprenticeship. The inputs do not name another Michigan school route for this trade.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 310 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $47,080. US median wage: $53,140. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +36.5%; about 4,200 openings a year in the US. The numbers show the fastest projected growth in this list, but Michigan's current workforce is small.
**AI and automation.** Software can help with system design, permits, shading analysis, and monitoring. Physical installation, roof work, and site problem-solving still need installers. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Electricians; Wind turbine service technicians; Carpenters.

### Operating engineers and other construction equipment operators (47-2073)
**The work.** Operating engineers and construction equipment operators run excavators, dozers, loaders, cranes, and similar machines. Work includes moving earth, lifting materials, digging, grading, and supporting road or building projects. The job is usually outdoors, in a cab or around heavy equipment, with weather, dust, and safety rules.
**Good fit if you...**

- Are careful around workers and underground utilities.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training. Union apprenticeship: International Union of Operating Engineers (IUOE); the report names IUOE Local 324 apprenticeship. WCC offers nothing for heavy equipment operation.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 9,500 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $62,690. US median wage: $59,850. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +4.6%; about 39,200 openings a year in the US. The numbers show steady demand tied to construction activity and worker replacement.
**AI and automation.** GPS grade control and machine-guidance software can improve accuracy. Site judgment, setup, and complex machine control still need operators. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Carpenters; Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers.

### Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers (53-3032)
**The work.** Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers transport goods using large trucks, often on long routes or regular delivery schedules. Work includes vehicle inspections, securing loads, following hours-of-service rules, and handling paperwork or electronic logs. Conditions include long sitting periods, traffic, docks, and tight delivery windows.
**Good fit if you...**
- Like driving and working independently.
- Can follow safety and log rules.
- Are okay with long hours away from home or early shifts.
**Ways in (Michigan).** BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and short-term on-the-job training. WCC offers no CDL or truck-driving program. The union lists do not enumerate a specific truck-driving apprenticeship. Other routes named in the report: Michigan Works! agencies fund CDL training at approved schools and sponsor registered truck driver apprenticeships; the report names Oakland NEXT: Truck Driver and a Lake Michigan College CDL-A cohort as examples.
**What the numbers say.** Michigan: about 60,800 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $57,180. US median wage: $58,640. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +3.8%; about 214,500 openings a year in the US. The numbers show a very large occupation with many openings, mainly from replacement needs.
**AI and automation.** Software can plan routes, manage logs, and support safety systems. Loading, inspections, and complex driving conditions still require human drivers. This is a judgment, not a measurement.
**Related trades.** Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists; Operating engineers and other construction equipment operators; Automotive service technicians and mechanics.

# Part 3: Trades the BLS data cannot count

These are real ways to earn a living with your hands that do not show up as their own row in the BLS tables. Worker counts for them have to come from other sources, which are not collected yet.

- **Farrier** (shoes horses): BLS has no occupation code for it. Counts would come from farrier associations and state agriculture records.
- **EV-charger installer**: done today by electricians and electrical installers; no separate BLS row.
- **Piano tuner and technician**: inside BLS "musical instrument repairers and tuners"; the trade association keeps its own member list.
- **Arborist** (tree climbing, pruning, removal): inside BLS "tree trimmers and pruners"; the common credential is the International Society of Arboriculture certificate.
- **Terrazzo and mosaic workers, restoration masons, sprinkler fitters, pipeliners**: crafts named by the building-trades unions (see the union lists) that BLS folds into broader rows.
