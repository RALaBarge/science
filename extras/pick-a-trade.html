Washtenaw Community College (WCC)

WCC is in Ann Arbor (wccnet.edu). This part comes from a survey of its 2026-2027 catalog and union-partnership pages. The big finding: for the biggest building trades (electrician, plumber and pipefitter, bricklayer, ironworker) WCC is the classroom and college-credit partner; the union and employer run the apprenticeship itself. WCC runs its own programs for HVAC, welding, machining, auto, industrial electronics, construction technology and many health technician careers.

Costs in the tables are arithmetic (credits times the 2026-27 in-district tuition plus fee), not a published total, and leave out books, tools, lab fees and exam fees. Wages and openings are the figures WCC shows on its own program pages for Michigan.

Is tuition free for in-district students? No. WCC's in-district rate is the lowest in the state and is frozen for 2026-27 (about $99 per credit in tuition, per WCC's tuition announcement), but it is not free; the cost column above includes a per-credit fee, which is why it uses $114. The one free group is Washtenaw County residents 65 and older. People who live outside the county but work 30 or more hours a week for an employer in it, or who own and live on property in it, can apply for the in-district rate (WCC tuition discounts). State aid such as the Michigan Reconnect and Michigan Achievement scholarships and the Tuition Incentive Program can cover tuition for students who qualify; ask WCC financial aid.

Electricians (47-2111)

The work. Electricians install, repair, and maintain wiring, fixtures, controls, and power systems in homes, businesses, factories, and construction sites. The work includes reading plans, running conduit, connecting panels, testing circuits, and troubleshooting faults. Conditions can include tight spaces, heights, outdoor weather, and live or locked-out equipment. Safety rules and electrical codes shape most tasks.

Good fit if you...

  • Like figuring out why a system is not working.
  • Are careful with safety and detail.
  • Can work with your hands in awkward spaces.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and an apprenticeship. Union apprenticeship: International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW); locally, IBEW Local 252 / Ann Arbor Electrical JATC. WCC offers credit only for electricians, not a standalone electrician program; the credit pathways are Apprentice Completion (CTAC) and Journeyman Industrial (APJPIM).

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 23,530 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $76,270. US median wage: $63,190. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +9.2%; about 72,700 openings a year in the US. Worker counts are wage-and-salary jobs and projections include the self-employed, so the two will not match.

AI and automation. Software can help with load calculations, code checks, drawings, and diagnostics. Robots are less able to handle routing wire through existing buildings, bending conduit around obstacles, and troubleshooting unusual faults. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Related trades. Electrical power-line installers and repairers; Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers; Solar photovoltaic installers; Industrial machinery mechanics.

Tags. mixed, crew, moderate work; focus: build, fix; uses: math, troubleshoot, detail; conditions: danger, tight-spaces, heights, noise; way in: apprenticeship.

Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers (49-9021)

The work. HVACR mechanics and installers set up, repair, and maintain heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration systems in homes, stores, plants, and other buildings. Work includes testing controls, replacing parts, checking refrigerant charge, and troubleshooting comfort or cooling problems. Conditions can include rooftops, tight mechanical spaces, hot or cold areas, and handling refrigerants.

Good fit if you...

  • Like mechanical and electrical troubleshooting.
  • Can work in tight or high places.
  • Are careful with refrigerant and safety rules.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and long-term on-the-job training. Union apprenticeship: United Association (UA), which covers HVACR service technicians; WCC provides classroom and credit for UA apprenticeships. WCC programs: HVAC and Refrigeration - Residential (CTHVRR), HVAC and Refrigeration - Commercial Trade (CVHVCT), and HVAC and Refrigeration (APHVCR). The residential certificate prepares for the third-class refrigeration licensure exam.

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 12,590 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $60,850. US median wage: $61,010. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +10.9%; about 40,600 openings a year in the US. The numbers show steady demand for workers who can install and service climate-control equipment.

AI and automation. Software can help with diagnostics, controls, energy tuning, and fault codes. Physical work such as running line sets, replacing compressors, and working in tight spaces still needs people. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Related trades. Electricians; Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters; Industrial machinery mechanics.

Tags. mixed, crew, moderate work; focus: build, fix; uses: troubleshoot, math, tech; conditions: tight-spaces, hot-cold, danger, noise; way in: certificate or short program.

Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters (47-2152)

The work. Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters install and repair pipe systems for water, gas, drainage, steam, and process fluids. Work includes measuring, cutting, threading, joining pipe, and testing systems for leaks. Settings range from homes to commercial buildings, plants, and construction sites, often in cramped or dirty conditions.

Good fit if you...

  • Like hands-on problem solving.
  • Can work in tight spaces and on call.
  • Are careful with code and safety requirements.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and an apprenticeship. Union apprenticeship: United Association (UA); locally, Greater Michigan UA Local 190 JATC. WCC offers credit only for this trade, not a standalone plumbing program; Apprentice Completion (CTAC) and Journeyman Industrial (APJPIM) are the named credit pathways.

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 14,120 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $80,190. US median wage: $63,800. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +6.8%; about 42,000 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to a well-paid Michigan trade with a solid number of openings.

AI and automation. Software can help with layout, code checking, and diagnostics, and prefabrication can reduce some on-site labor. Cutting, fitting, and repairing pipe in existing buildings are hard to automate fully. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Related trades. Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers; Electricians.

Tags. mixed, crew, moderate work; focus: build, fix; uses: math, troubleshoot; conditions: tight-spaces, dirty, danger, noise; way in: apprenticeship.

Industrial machinery mechanics (49-9041)

The work. Industrial machinery mechanics maintain, diagnose, and repair production equipment such as conveyors, robots, hydraulic and pneumatic systems, and machine tools. Work often happens in factories and plants, where mechanics may remove parts, align components, and test repairs. Conditions can include noise, grease, confined spaces, and scheduled shutdowns.

Good fit if you...

  • Like taking machines apart and fixing them.
  • Can use electrical, mechanical, and computer skills together.
  • Are willing to learn many types of equipment.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. Union list: International Union of Operating Engineers (IUOE), which includes mechanics. The Michigan Regional Council of Carpenters and Millwrights runs an apprenticeship for millwrights, who install and align industrial machinery. WCC programs: Industrial Electronics Technology (CFIET), Robotics Technician (CTROBT), Automation Specialist (CVAMSP), and Mechatronics - Robotics and Automated Systems (APMRAS).

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 17,310 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $64,250. US median wage: $64,520. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +17.8%; about 44,400 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to strong projected growth and many openings in manufacturing maintenance.

AI and automation. Software can monitor equipment, predict failures, and guide diagnostics. Robots can do some repetitive tasks, but repairing and installing machinery usually requires hands-on judgment. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Related trades. Machinists; Welders, cutters, solderers, and brazers; Heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers.

Tags. indoors, crew, moderate work; focus: fix; uses: troubleshoot, math, tech; conditions: noise, dirty, tight-spaces, danger; way in: on-the-job (months to years).

Automotive service technicians and mechanics (49-3023)

The work. Automotive service technicians and mechanics inspect, diagnose, and repair cars, trucks, and other light vehicles. Work includes engines, brakes, steering, electrical systems, batteries, and computer-controlled components. Most work is in shops or dealerships, often with lifts, scan tools, greasy parts, and time targets.

Good fit if you...

  • Like diagnosing problems with tools and computers.
  • Can work around vehicles and small parts.
  • Are willing to keep learning as vehicles change.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: postsecondary nondegree award and short-term on-the-job training. We did not find a specific apprenticeship for automotive service technicians. WCC programs: Automotive Services Technician (CTASVT), Advanced Automotive Services Technician (CVASV2), Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Safety and Fundamentals (CTEHSF), and Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Service Technician (CTEHST).

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 22,280 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $54,120. US median wage: $50,620. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +5%; about 66,200 openings a year in the US. The numbers show a large occupation with many openings, mostly to replace workers.

AI and automation. Scan tools and software can find faults, guide repairs, and calibrate driver-assist systems. Physical repair, disassembly, and judgment about worn parts still need technicians. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Related trades. Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists; Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers; Industrial machinery mechanics.

Tags. indoors, crew, moderate work; focus: fix; uses: troubleshoot, tech; conditions: dirty, noise, tight-spaces; way in: certificate or short program.

Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists (49-3031)

The work. Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists repair and maintain heavy vehicles, buses, fleets, and diesel engines. Work can include brakes, electrical systems, fuel systems, transmissions, and preventive maintenance inspections. The job is often in fleet garages or roadside settings, with dirty, noisy, and time-sensitive conditions.

Good fit if you...

  • Like big engines and heavy-duty systems.
  • Can follow safety rules around large vehicles.
  • Are okay with on-road breakdowns or shift work.

Ways in (Michigan). BLS typical entry: high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. We did not find a specific apprenticeship for this trade. WCC does not offer a standalone diesel/heavy truck program; Automotive Services Technician (CTASVT) includes diesel exposure.

What the numbers say. Michigan: about 9,020 workers (OEWS, May 2025), median wage $61,420. US median wage: $61,770. Projected change 2025 to 2035: +3.6%; about 24,400 openings a year in the US. The numbers point to steady work and ongoing openings to keep fleets moving.

AI and automation. Telematics and diagnostic software can flag problems before they become breakdowns. Hands-on repair of engines, brakes, and hydraulics is not easy to automate. This is a judgment, not a measurement.

Trades the BLS data cannot count

Real ways to earn a living with your hands that do not show up as their own row in the BLS tables. Worker counts for them have to come from other sources, which are not collected yet.

  • Farrier (shoes horses): no BLS occupation code. Counts would come from farrier associations and state agriculture records.
  • EV-charger installer: done today by electricians and electrical installers; no separate BLS row.
  • Piano tuner and technician: inside BLS "musical instrument repairers and tuners"; the trade association keeps its own member list.
  • Arborist (tree climbing, pruning, removal): inside BLS "tree trimmers and pruners"; the common credential is the International Society of Arboriculture certificate.
  • Terrazzo and mosaic workers, restoration masons, sprinkler fitters, pipeliners: crafts named by the building-trades unions that BLS folds into broader rows.
