# Grok review of the K-5 units (x-ai/grok-4.7, 2026-11-03)

Saved in full and unedited. Section 1 is the content review (the text fixes were applied to the units; what is still open is in TODO.md). Sections 2-4 are the game, diagram and build-order plans, parked until the content is checked.

## 1. Review

Severity is about wrong science, a standard verb the activity cannot earn, or a child getting hurt. Quiz giveaways and tight pacing are lower. I am not treating a simplification as an error when the write-up hedges it and the grade standard does not need the mechanism.

### High

- **5.3 Concepts 2–3.** Matter and energy are drawn as one trip. The food-web line says arrows show “where matter and energy go.” Matter cycles; energy does not. Food energy leaves as heat and motion and does not return to sunlight. Quiz 3 then scores “arrows show where matter and energy move” as if those were the same path. The plant diagram makes the same mistake: sunlight is an input arrow beside water and air, and “sugar out” sends the plant’s new mass out of the plant. Sunlight is not matter. Sugar is mostly what the plant becomes. This will undo 5-LS1-1, 5-PS3-1, and 5-LS2-1 if a game copies it.
- **5.4 Concept 2.** The 100-square graph is three bins: 97 salt, 2 frozen, 1 “liquid fresh.” There is no groundwater reservoir. 5-ESS2-2 asks for amounts in reservoirs so students can see that most fresh water is ice or underground and that lakes and rivers are a sliver. The misconception list says that. The graph does not. “Slightly more complex” is the wrong hedge. Completing this activity does not produce the evidence the PE asks for.
- **1-C Concept 3 and Quiz 3.** 1-ESS1-2 only asks students to relate daylight amount to time of year. The analogy says Earth “tips” so that “in summer our part tips toward the Sun.” That reads as the axis rocking back and forth. The quiz then makes that sentence the scored cause. Michigan connection child text, “bright summer nights,” is false: summer evenings stay light; the night is not bright. 3.2 correctly refuses to teach the cause. 1-C teaches a bad one, then assesses it.
- **5.1 Concept 5, safety note.** Conservation needs a closed system. The safety line says not to seal the bag tightly. An open bag loses CO₂, mass drops, and the demo “proves” the misconception that gas has no weight. A tight bag can pop and spray vinegar. The balloon-on-a-straw “air has mass” demo is also easy to lose to buoyancy. The provided 82 g = 82 g table hides both failures from the page and not from the room.
- **K-B, 1-A, 1-C kits.** Coin cells for primary flashlights. Button cells are a swallowing and burn hazard. K-B already allows a lamp or window. Do not specify coin cells in K–2.
- **4.5, whole unit.** Five PEs in six 30-minute sessions. Concept 4 never states one real effect of gas, coal, wind, or solar, so 4-ESS3-1 is claimed without the information students are supposed to combine. The map “dataset” is already the conclusion (“mountains run in long ranges”). Quiz 4, “most water is salty,” is not taught in any 4.5 concept. It is 5.4 content, dropped in early.

### Medium-high

- **Standards verbs vs backup tables.** 2-B says 2-LS2-1 is fully earned by the bean test **or** the provided table. K-ESS2-1, 1-ESS1-1, 1-ESS1-2, and 2-LS4-1 do the same with calendars, sky logs, and picture counts. “Plan and conduct” and “use observations” are not “read our numbers.” The backup is fine as a backup. It is not full coverage when it is the only path. K-B and K-C are more honest about K-PS3-2 and K-ESS3-3. Keep those conditions on the page.
- **3.1 Concepts 1–2.** “Balanced forces keep motion the same” is right. The activities only show a tie at rest and a push that starts a car. They never show a moving object with balanced forces keeping its motion. The dot table is 0, 10, 20, 30 cm at 0–3 s: no friction, predict 40 cm. A real ball will not do that. The text also says friction slows objects. The guaranteed data and the floor test disagree, so 3-PS2-2 is not earned by either one alone.
- **5.1 engineering task.** Magnet, water, and a filter cannot separate salt as a solid. Evaporation is missing. “The salt is still there” is told, not shown. That is the misconception the unit exists to fix.
- **4.3 Concept 1.** Rope, sound, and light are listed together as waves that move objects. 4-PS4-1 is a mechanical-wave model. Light does not bob a cork. A later sentence, “waves can be rope, sound, and light,” glues the error in place.
- **4.2 safety.** Foil across a coin cell gets hot. The light-up card task is exactly how students make that short. No warning, no “if it is hot, disconnect.”
- **5.3 decomposer table.** 35 g leaf pieces + 65 g soil = 100 g. The sentence under it says matter returns to soil **and air**. The table leaves no mass for air.

### Medium

- **1-B Concept 3.** Big idea: parents feed, protect, teach, and stay near their young. That is not a pattern across animals. The caveat is buried in risks. 1-LS1-2 needs examples, not a rule. The rain-hat-like-a-leaf build is also a weak function match; the burr is the one that can be tested.
- **False spiral lines.** 1-A says vibration and material tests “return from earlier units.” They do not. 1-B says biomimicry returns. It does not. 1-C says Sky Patterns returns. K-B was weather, not sun/moon/star patterns. 2-C says students already tested wind and water erosion. They have not. The duplicated “You did this before / what is new: You did this before” is the same paste.
- **2-C glossary.** “Water: liquid found in lakes, rivers, oceans, ice, and snow.” Ice and snow are the solids the lesson just sorted. The definition cancels Concept 4.
- **2-A Concept 3 and rainproof table.** The real-world example for “take pieces apart and rebuild” is recycling cans, which usually means melting, not 2-PS1-3. Plastic wrap and foil are both dry on all three trials, and the table still names plastic “Best.” The data do not pick a winner. Quiz never checks 2-PS1-2 or 2-PS1-3.
- **2-B Concept 3.** “Forest had 6 kinds. Dune had 3 kinds” is the card set, repeated from K-C, and it is written as a fact about habitats. Picture-set size is not 2-LS4-1 evidence. Trout as the pond animal is the wrong local match (cold streams and lakes).
- **3.2 Concept 2 and Detroit table.** A wind vane points into the wind; “wind direction” means where it comes from. The write-up does not say that, and a paper arrow without a tail points the wrong way. The monthly figures match Detroit average **highs** (Jan ~32°F, Jul ~83°F) but are labeled typical temperature. Means are roughly 8–10°F lower. Seasonal shape is fine; the label is not.
- **4.4 Concept 3.** Sight catches at 9–12 cm; sound at 12–14 cm. Farther down the ruler means slower, and the page never says so. The sound cue is also a worse fair test than the visual drop: the stimulus timing is not the same event. 4-LS1-2 does not need this race. The model does.
- **4.5 Concepts 3–4.** Students are handed the map patterns they were supposed to find. Energy cards, as written, have no effects to combine. 4-ESS3-2 is a third erosion-tray repeat (see below), not a new Earth process.
- **5.2.** The paraphrase “Closer stars look brighter” is false for stars compared with each other. The Sun-vs-other-stars claim in the big idea is the one the PE allows. Shadow evidence is length only. 5-ESS1-2 also requires direction. “Arrows pointing toward ground” does not yet show “toward Earth’s center.”
- **K–2 quizzes.** Distractors restate the misconception, often with the reason inside the option (“because plants eat soil,” “because one try is enough”). Acceptable as a read-aloud rehearsal. Weak as a check. Grade 3–5 items are generally one-keyed and usable.
- **2-C kit.** Blowing sand through straws: shared straws, sand in eyes and noses. One straw each, or a fan/pour. Do not blow sand.
- **Spiral collision on the same beans and the same tray.** Beans carry 2-LS2-1, then 3-LS3-2, then 5-LS1-1. Erosion spoon-counts recur in 2-C, 3.2, 4.5, and 5.4 with different numbers (8/2/5, then 12/5/7, then 12/4/7). Unless each page says what is new, students will treat one messy experiment as the finding, and they will use “it grew in the light” as the grade 5 answer to “where did the mass come from?”

### Low

- **K-B Concept 3.** Lake-effect as a K read-aloud mechanism. Not false. Not the standard. Cut it.
- **K-C Concept 1.** “Plants make food” is ahead of the evidence. “Needs water, light, and air” is the K claim. Soil-as-food is already handled.
- **1-C Concept 2, child text.** “The Moon’s shape changes” fights the big idea’s “seems to change.”
- **2-B Concept 1.** A dark box changes temperature as well as light. The fourth dataset row changes two variables after the big idea says change one.
- **3.1 opener.** Soup-tasting fair test in a forces unit. Hygiene, and it is not 3-PS2 evidence. Keep magnets off Chromebooks, as written.
- **3.4 Concept 4.** “Needs water to breathe” slides into “fish breathe water.” Gills only work in water.
- **4.2 kit list.** Brass fasteners are in the switch try-it and not in the kit.
- **4.4.** “Lungs move air” skips the diaphragm. Acceptable if the diagram does not show lungs sucking on their own.
- **K-A.** Stopper data are “stopped / did not,” not a speed measure. At K, stopping is a visible speed change. Not an error.
- **K2.0 if skipped, and Unit 0 also skipped.** K-2-ETS1-1 is then absent. Already documented. Leave it on the coverage table.

### None found

- No keyed quiz item has two defensible answers. Near-misses only: 2-A Quiz 1 (color is a property; “only by color” still loses to A), the rainproof task above (two materials meet the goal; that is a task flaw, not a double-keyed item), 5.3 Quiz 3 (A is clear; “B is not enough” is a messy key, and A is the scientifically fused claim criticized above).
- **K2.0:** no disciplinary science error. Practices unit, correctly not claiming ETS1-2 or ETS1-3.
- **K-A:** no important science error. Push toward you / pull toward you is the right K meaning. Gravity-as-pull is correctly left optional.
- **4-PS3-2 split** across 4.1 and 4.2 is honest if both units are taught.
- K-PS3-2 and K-ESS3-3 caveats in the write-up are right. They become overclaims only if the generated page drops the condition.
- Petoskey stone as a shallow-sea coral fossil: correct. Coat does not make heat: correct. Dissolving is not a new substance: correct. Particles do not get bigger when heated: correct as stated. Weathering breaks / erosion moves: correct in the 4.5 text. LED works one way: correct. Daylight strips of about 9 / 12 / 15 squares fit southern Michigan. Severe-weather sorts are framed as calm preparation, not spectacle. No other safety issues beyond those named.

## 2. Games

Each file must **copy** engine code inline. No shared script, no network. Run it in a shadow root. Cap layout at about 1024×600. K–2: targets at least 64 px, tap or drag, no required reading, no timer, no lives, no “loudest score.” `speechSynthesis` is optional and must not be required. Seed fair-test and signal levels so a teacher demo matches the class.

Do not reuse **Gravity Duel**, **Football Duel**, or **Balance Duel** for these units. A race rewards “harder/heavier wins,” which is a listed misconception. Do reuse **Mystery Machine** (fair test), **Laser Bounce** (reflect), **Short Circuit** (loop), and **Phase Runner** (particle spacing), with the fail-safes below added.

An arcade game is the wrong tool where I say so.

### K2.0 — wrong tool
- **Name:** Notice Tray (not Notice Walk Detective).
- **Mechanic:** Six backyard objects. Tap one, stamp one detail (smooth, bumpy, spot), and drop a tally mark. A second stamp on the same object is allowed and counts. Then two big cards: one testable picture, one not-yet picture.
- **Child does:** Taps, stamps, sorts two question pictures.
- **Evidence:** ETS1-1 only in the weak sense of “notice, then a question we can test.” A screen cannot earn the PE. The tray of real objects does.
- **Fail-safe:** No speed score, so the loudest/fastest does not win. A drawing stamp counts as data, not only a number. A beaker icon is not the “science” button. One tap is not complete.
- **Input:** Tap. No reading.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Big Sort. Interim only.

### K-A
- **Name:** Push Pit.
- **Mechanic:** Three big strength buttons, four direction arrows, a wind icon, and a pull handle. One run changes one of those. The car leaves tile marks and stops when a rub mark or a wall is hit, not because a “push juice” meter hits zero. Stopper mode: place one block, run three times, see three stop dots.
- **Child does:** Sets one control, taps Go, compares tile counts or stop dots.
- **Evidence:** K-PS2-1 strength or direction comparison; K-PS2-2 repeated design data. Tile counts are the data.
- **Fail-safe:** Always show what stopped it. Wind can push with no hands. A harder push into a wall does not go farther, so “bigger always wins” fails. Pull only moves the car toward the figure. Second variable locks. One trial cannot finish the level.
- **Input:** Big buttons. No reading.
- **Size:** M first build, then S. **Engine:** Push Arena.

### K-B
- **Name:** Sun Safe Sort.
- **Mechanic:** Drag black paper, white paper, pavement, and grass into sun or shade; a face-thermometer rises more for dark-in-sun. Shade shows rays stopped, not a cold-maker. Second board: forecast icon with a “maybe” mark, then coat / inside / stay-off-roads pictures. A five-day strip can be marked with weather stamps.
- **Child does:** Sorts pictures and stamps a short calendar.
- **Evidence:** K-PS3-1 and the shade idea in K-PS3-2. The calendar strip is only practice for K-ESS2-1; real or class data still have to exist. K-ESS3-2 is “which preparation,” not a forecast the child wrote.
- **Fail-safe:** Shade never fills a snowflake. Dark-in-sun gets warmer, not cooler. Forecast does not flip the weather. Storm board is slow and has no damage animation. No panic face as the “correct” busy answer.
- **Input:** Drag to big bins. No reading. Adult reads four words if needed.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Big Sort.

### K-C
- **Name:** Home Builder.
- **Mechanic:** Choose robin, trout, deer, or bean. Drag food, water, shelter, and space into pond, forest, or dune. The animal stays only if the place supplies its needs. Before/after pictures: beaver dam, worm soil, litter. A helper round is before/after counts, not a substitute for the real litter pick or planting.
- **Child does:** Drags need icons and taps “who changed this place?”
- **Evidence:** K-ESS3-1 model. K-LS1-1 pattern of needs. K-ESS2-2 only if the child picks the changer and the clue. The game cannot earn K-ESS3-3.
- **Fail-safe:** Soil is not a food icon for the plant. A bed icon does not satisfy habitat. People and beavers can both be the changer. Litter is a harm, not “nothing.”
- **Input:** Drag, big pictures. No reading.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Scene & Web. Until that exists, a Big Sort animal-to-place match is the interim and does not earn the model PE.

### 1-A
- **Name:** See-and-Send.
- **Mechanic:** Pluck a band (WebAudio tone); rice dots move only while it sounds. Mystery box: lamp off, object gone; lamp on, object there. Drag clear, wax, card, or foil into a beam. Then tap a three-beat flash or drum pattern; a partner repeats it. Wrong pattern does not open the door.
- **Child does:** Plucks, toggles light, drags one material, taps a pattern.
- **Evidence:** Directly practices 1-PS4-1, 1-PS4-2, 1-PS4-3, and the pattern half of 1-PS4-4. The built device in class still earns the engineering PE.
- **Fail-safe:** No flying “sound thing.” Eyes do not emit. Blocked light stops and leaves a small warm spot, it does not vanish. Foil bounces; it is not “through.” A random flash fails.
- **Input:** Big taps and one drag. One-word labels, adult can read.
- **Size:** M, or S if Laser Bounce is copied for the beam. **Engine:** Path & Signal.

### 1-B
- **Name:** Job Match.
- **Mechanic:** One highlighted part, three job pictures. Then one human job (stay dry, pick up bits, keep warm) and three nature parts; only the part with that job passes a tiny test. A third board shows adult and young with one “like” mark and one “different” mark. Parent-care is three separate stories, not one rule.
- **Child does:** Taps the job, then the part to copy, then like/different.
- **Evidence:** 1-LS1-1 function match, not the build. 1-LS3-1 comparison. 1-LS1-2 as examples. The hands-on helper still has to be built and tested.
- **Fail-safe:** “Copy the whole animal” is a choice and fails the test. An exact-copy fawn fails the different check. A “parents always teach” card is not offered. Decoration is a job choice and fails.
- **Input:** Tap. Minimal words.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Big Sort.

### 1-C
- **Name:** Day Spin.
- **Mechanic:** A fixed lamp, a ball, a sticker “you.” Child taps Spin. The sticker enters light, then dark. Stars stay in the sky; a blue wash hides them until the wash is tapped off. Moon cards sort as “looks like,” four cards. Long and short daylight strips drag onto summer and winter pictures. No tilt control exists.
- **Child does:** Spins, orders, matches strips.
- **Evidence:** Pattern descriptions for 1-ESS1-1 and daylight amount for 1-ESS1-2. Does not replace looking at the sky or the strip log. Does not teach cause of seasons.
- **Fail-safe:** The Sun never moves and never turns off. Stars do not disappear when the wash is removed. Moon cards say “looks like,” not “the Moon changed shape.” No closer-to-Sun season ending. No tilt quiz.
- **Input:** One big Spin button, drag four cards. No reading.
- **Size:** S–M. **Engine:** Graph & Sky for the spin; Big Sort for moon order and strips.

### 2-A
- **Name:** Dry Note.
- **Mechanic:** Pick paper, cloth, foil, or plastic. Three sprays, same count, locked. Note shows dry or wet. If two stay dry, both meet the goal; a second criterion (bends, or holds a fold) is required before one is “best.” Separate board: same four pieces make two shapes; piece count stays put. Ice/butter get an Undo button; egg/toast do not.
- **Child does:** Tests, rebuilds, tries Undo.
- **Evidence:** 2-PS1-2 data choice, 2-PS1-1 property use, 2-PS1-3 piece count, 2-PS1-4 reversible or not.
- **Fail-safe:** Size is not a sort key on this board. Undo fails on egg and toast, so “all changes undo” fails. Heating changes shape, not just a “warm” color. Recycling and melting are not in this game.
- **Input:** Big pictures; short words OK for grade 2.
- **Size:** S once Fair Test exists. **Engine:** Fair Test Lab, plus a Big Sort undo board.

### 2-B — growth game is the wrong tool
- **Name:** Pollen Run, plus a bean-claim viewer. Do not animate a 14-day plant in ten seconds.
- **Mechanic:** Drag a bee or a sock across three flowers or burrs. Powder or seeds move on at least two of three tries, or the level does not pass. The bean screen shows three cups and asks which pair is a fair comparison, then reveals heights, including a small pale sprout in the dark.
- **Child does:** Drags the mover; taps the fair pair; reads or hears the heights.
- **Evidence:** 2-LS2-2 model function. 2-LS2-1 only as “which comparison is fair,” not as the investigation. Habitat counts are omitted on purpose.
- **Fail-safe:** Dark sprout is small, not zero, and the soil pile does not shrink. Honey is not the win condition. Seeds do not only fall at the flower. “Picture set had 6 cards” is never voiced as “dunes have less life.”
- **Input:** Drag. Short labels.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Scene & Web for pollen; Fair Test for the bean claim.

### 2-C
- **Name:** Guard the Sand.
- **Mechanic:** Same pour or same puff, three times, on bare sand, grass, and stones. Finger-width marks. Child picks which guard moved less. Fast/slow and solid/liquid are Big Sort skins, not this game. A landform drag (lake, river, hill, dune) can sit on the Scene engine later; it is not required to earn the compare-solutions PE.
- **Child does:** Runs the same pour, compares marks, taps the better guard.
- **Evidence:** 2-ESS2-1 comparison with repeated trials. Does not earn 2-ESS1-1 or 2-ESS2-3; those stay sorts.
- **Fail-safe:** “More wind” is not a guard and cannot be selected as the solution. Bare sand loses. A badge says the tray is smaller than a real place. Wind is moving-air lines, not clouds. Water icons include ice, snow, and underground, not only a lake.
- **Input:** Big buttons and picture cards. Little reading.
- **Size:** S on Fair Test. **Engine:** Fair Test Lab. This is the only erosion game. 3.2, 4.5, and 5.4 get a new question banner, not a new sim.

### 3.1
- **Name:** Arrow Tug.
- **Mechanic:** Left and right arrows on a puck. Equal arrows: a resting puck stays; a moving puck keeps going. Unequal arrows change speed or direction. Friction can be switched on; the dot spacing then tightens, and the prediction must follow that pattern, not the 10 cm table. Magnet mode: clip follows, foil and penny do not; two poles repel; farther means weaker.
- **Child does:** Sets arrows, predicts the next dot, tries objects, moves a clip without touching it.
- **Evidence:** 3-PS2-1 balanced and unbalanced, including motion unchanged. 3-PS2-2 prediction from the pattern they actually set. 3-PS2-3 distance question. 3-PS2-4 is still the class design problem; the game only shows the scientific idea.
- **Fail-safe:** Continuing motion with equal arrows is a required level, so “needs a force to keep moving” fails. Magnet level includes foil. A huge push with friction on does not “go forever.” Touch is not required for the magnet pull.
- **Input:** Drag arrows; click objects. Keyboard optional. Short sentences OK.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Push Arena.

### 3.2
- **Name:** Month Bars.
- **Mechanic:** Drag twelve high-temperature values onto a graph labeled “typical high,” not “the temperature.” Match the shape to a cold-winter region card. One odd cold July day cannot relabel the climate. Barrier mode uses the shared erosion skin, then a claim chip: “reduces” is available, “stops all floods” is not.
- **Child does:** Builds a graph, matches a region, completes a claim.
- **Evidence:** 3-ESS2-1 representation. 3-ESS2-2 combine-and-describe, if region cards have real ranges. 3-ESS3-1 merit claim that includes a limit.
- **Fail-safe:** Weather and climate are two labeled strips, one day vs many years. No “closer to the Sun” season card. Barrier never clears the hazard icon. Wind arrow, if shown, is labeled “from.”
- **Input:** Drag and tap. Grade 3 reading.
- **Size:** M for the engine, S for this skin. **Engine:** Graph & Sky. Barrier: Fair Test banner only.

### 3.3
- **Name:** Same but Not Same.
- **Mechanic:** Order four pictures for frog, bean, and beetle; the layouts are different. Stamp Begin, Grow, Reproduce, Die on each. Match offspring to parent; an exact duplicate is rejected. Sun and shade beans stay labeled “bean,” with different heights.
- **Child does:** Orders, stamps shared stages, rejects the exact copy.
- **Evidence:** 3-LS1-1 models. 3-LS3-1 resemblance plus variation. 3-LS3-2 environment changes the look, not the kind.
- **Fail-safe:** One shared template cannot pass all three cycles. Exact copy fails. Shade plant does not become a new species card. No gene, no “evolution” meter.
- **Input:** Drag and tap.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Big Sort order mode, plus a small Graph bar for heights.

### 3.4
- **Name:** Who Can Live Here.
- **Mechanic:** Place frog, beach grass, deer, fish. Each must be tagged survives well, less well, or not at all, with one reason. A change card arrives. A solution will not submit until one benefit and one cost are both chosen. Fossil cards match to “shallow sea,” “wet land,” or “we cannot tell” so fish-fossil-means-lake can fail. Group card shows more eyes and less food per animal.
- **Child does:** Tags fit, picks a solution, names a cost.
- **Evidence:** Argument material for 3-LS4-3 and 3-LS4-4. Fossil match for 3-LS4-1. Group trade-off for 3-LS2-1. Fur level is labeled “made-up model,” with some thin-fur survivors, for 3-LS4-2.
- **Fail-safe:** “Helps every member” fails the group card. “No cost” cannot submit. Not every variation wins. Fossil is a stone picture, not a living bone. No one-winter evolution animation.
- **Input:** Drag and tap. Short claim frame.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Scene & Web.

### 4.1
- **Name:** Farther Hit.
- **Mechanic:** Lock mass or lock ramp height. Predict which of three distances the block moves, then run three trials. An energy bar leaves the marble, most to the block’s motion, a little to heat/sound. A coat level: heat arrow from body outward slows under the coat; the coat has no heat source.
- **Child does:** Changes one variable, predicts, tests, watches the bar.
- **Evidence:** 4-PS3-1 speed compared at constant mass. 4-PS3-3 prediction about a collision. Heat and sound transfer for part of 4-PS3-2.
- **Fail-safe:** Faster is not “less energy.” The bar does not vanish and does not all become block motion. Heat arrow never points cold to hot. Coat does not add energy. Heavier-vs-faster is a different level, so one push does not explain both.
- **Input:** Buttons and a three-choice predict strip.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Push Arena with a Fair Test lock. Do not skin Football Duel.

### 4.2
- **Name:** Loop Light.
- **Mechanic:** Copy Short Circuit. Place cell, foil, LED, switch. Light only if the loop is closed and the LED points the right way. Gap test: clip and foil light; string, plastic, wood do not. Current arrows keep circulating. Energy chip changes to light. If foil bridges the cell, the screen says hot, breaks the circuit, and does not score.
- **Child does:** Builds a loop, flips the LED, tests one material, closes a switch.
- **Evidence:** Electric-current transfer for 4-PS3-2 and a conversion device for 4-PS3-4. Class still builds the card.
- **Fail-safe:** One wire fails. Open switch fails. Plastic fails. “Current used up” fails because arrows continue while the energy chip changes. Wall outlet is not a piece. Short does not score.
- **Input:** Drag. Keyboard optional.
- **Size:** S if Short Circuit is copied; M if not. **Engine:** Path & Signal.

### 4.3
- **Name:** Two Signals.
- **Mechanic:** Rope or water wave only: two handles, amplitude and wavelength; cork moves more with a taller wave. Separate level: lamp, object, eye; the beam must be aimed into the eye. Signal level: same message by flash or tap; a bright-room seed and a noisy-room seed swap which one is clearer.
- **Child does:** Sets a wave, aims a mirror, sends one message two ways, picks which fit this room.
- **Evidence:** 4-PS4-1 mechanical model. 4-PS4-2 path into the eye. 4-PS4-3 compare two solutions under a constraint.
- **Fail-safe:** Light is not a cork-wave. Eye emits nothing. Vacuum jar: rice does not move. A pretty pattern with no code fails. Flashlight is not always best.
- **Input:** Drag handles and tap a pattern.
- **Size:** M. Mirror aim can be Laser Bounce. **Engine:** Path & Signal.

### 4.4 — arcade is the wrong tool
- **Name:** In Then Act.
- **Mechanic:** Match structure to job, inside and outside. Then three ordered slots: sense, brain, response. Deer, ball, and robin scenarios. Plants have no brain path. Do not score reaction speed.
- **Child does:** Matches parts, orders the three slots.
- **Evidence:** 4-LS1-1 job argument prompts. 4-LS1-2 model. The ruler drop stays in class, with a sentence on the page: farther catch means slower.
- **Fail-safe:** Hand → eye → brain does not lock in. Sense and response cannot occupy the same slot. “Decoration” fails. Outside-only set fails if the prompt says heart or tubes.
- **Input:** Drag into slots.
- **Size:** S. **Engine:** Big Sort with ordered slots.

### 4.5 — new shoreline game is the wrong tool
- **Name:** Layer Map.
- **Mechanic:** Peel three layers; shell fossil in a deep layer tags “once a sea,” with “usually older if undisturbed.” Map: tap one mountain chain, one broad plain, one connected lake chain; the labels are not pre-written. Resource card will not file until an effect chip (land, air, or water) is attached. Erosion test is the existing Guard the Sand skin with a shoreline banner.
- **Child does:** Peels, taps map patterns, attaches an effect.
- **Evidence:** 4-ESS1-1 layer-and-fossil reading. 4-ESS2-2 pattern finding. 4-ESS3-1 only if the effect chips say something real (mine scar, smoke, land used by turbines). 4-ESS2-1 and 4-ESS3-2 stay on the shared fair test.
- **Fail-safe:** Weathering tool is “break,” erosion tool is “move,” and swapping them fails. “No effect” is not a chip. Ocean-water quiz item is not in this game. Fossil is not a fresh bone.
- **Input:** Tap and drag.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Scene & Web, plus the existing Fair Test skin.

### 5.1 — Particle Dance is the wrong tool
- **Name:** Still There.
- **Mechanic:** Extend Phase Runner. About twenty dots, not a costume change: solid, liquid, gas change spacing and motion; the count stays on screen. Heat makes them move more, not grow. Dissolve: salt dots remain in the water; evaporate and they reappear. Sealed fizz keeps the balance level and shows a new gas dot type. Open bag drops the balance and is marked “leak,” not “gas weighs nothing.”
- **Child does:** Switches state, counts dots, evaporates, compares sealed and open.
- **Evidence:** 5-PS1-1 model. 5-PS1-2 conservation with a visible reason the live bag may fail. 5-PS1-3 property sort can be a short Big Sort. 5-PS1-4 new gas vs still-salt.
- **Fail-safe:** Dots do not enlarge on heat. Dissolving does not spawn a new substance. Melting stays “water.” Open bag explains a mass drop instead of rewarding it. Air-in-a-bag is a “takes space” press, not a float contest.
- **Input:** Slider and tap. Short labels.
- **Size:** M on Phase Runner; L from scratch. **Engine:** Phase Runner plus Graph bars for before/after mass.

### 5.2
- **Name:** Shadow Clock.
- **Mechanic:** Drag the Sun along an arc over a Michigan scene. Shadow length and direction both change: morning shadow west, noon north, afternoon east. Graph both. Gravity: drop, toss up, toss sideways; arrow on a globe points to the center; heavy and light balls fall together. A feather toggle is separate and labeled air. Star level: same lamp closer looks brighter; a second level lets a far lamp be intrinsically brighter so “closer always wins” fails. Night side of an orbit faces a different constellation card.
- **Child does:** Drags the Sun, graphs shadow, tosses, matches season cards.
- **Evidence:** 5-ESS1-2 length and direction, day/night, seasonal stars. 5-PS2-1 direction toward center. 5-ESS1-1 Sun vs other stars, without the false general rule.
- **Fail-safe:** Sun does not orbit Earth in the day/night model. Stars do not turn off; the night side turns. Heavier does not fall faster. Gravity works with the air toggle off. “Biggest star” is not the brightness answer.
- **Input:** Drag. Grade 5 labels.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Graph & Sky. Do not use Gravity Duel.

### 5.3 — feeding battle is the wrong tool
- **Name:** Sun to Soil.
- **Mechanic:** Two boards that cannot share arrows. Matter board: air and water add mass; minerals add a tiny dot; sunlight will not dock in the mass box and snaps to an energy rail. Energy board: Sun → plant → eater → heat puff, one direction. Web board: producer, consumer, decomposer. Remove one card and neighbors change. Decomposer split is soil **and** air, and the numbers must not sum to 100% in soil alone.
- **Child does:** Tries to put sunlight in the mass box, fails, builds two different arrow types, removes one population.
- **Evidence:** 5-LS1-1 matter sources. 5-PS3-1 food energy back to the Sun, ending in warmth and motion. 5-LS2-1 matter movement including decomposers. This is the interactive that earns the unit. The bean grower does not.
- **Fail-safe:** Sunlight cannot become mass. Package and refrigerator are not energy sources. “Plants eat soil” fails when soil barely changes. Dead matter does not vanish. Energy arrows cannot be bent into a cycle. Decay is not a “stop this” button.
- **Input:** Drag onto two labeled rails.
- **Size:** M. **Engine:** Scene & Web.

### 5.4
- **Name:** Four Spheres.
- **Mechanic:** One rain-on-a-hill scene. Child tags atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere, and biosphere; all four are required. Water grid is four reservoirs: ocean, ice, underground, lakes/rivers (lakes/rivers are a few cells, not a full 1% bin). Community card: rain garden, dune grass, or fertilizer rule; runoff drops; a flood or trade-off icon remains.
- **Child does:** Tags the scene, colors reservoirs, picks a solution and a limit.
- **Evidence:** 5-ESS2-1 interaction model. 5-ESS2-2 distribution the PE actually wants. 5-ESS3-1 obtain-and-combine only if the cards have a problem, a solution, and evidence; the game rehearses that, it does not replace the cards.
- **Fail-safe:** Spheres cannot be left unconnected. “Plenty of lake water” fails the grid. Rain garden does not remove the flood icon. One solution does not check every problem. Roots are a biosphere tag, so “only nonliving things change land” fails.
- **Input:** Tap tags, color a 10×10 grid.
- **Size:** S–M. **Engine:** Graph & Sky for the grid; Scene & Web for the tags. Erosion numbers: existing Fair Test banner, not a new game.

## 4. Build order

Text fixes come before skins that would freeze the error: no tilt game, no 97/2/1 grid, no food web with one arrow type, no frictionless “predict 40 cm” as the only 3.1 level, no coin cells in K–2 files.

1. **Big Sort engine, with the K-B sun/shade/safety skin and the 2-C fast/slow skin.** Most units need it. K–2 can use it with no reading. It is the only way the text-first pages get interaction quickly.
2. **1-C day/night spin, with stars under the sky wash.** Small, and it is the highest-severity K–2 science fix. Ship it as the first Graph & Sky piece, not as a season-cause game.
3. **K-A Push Pit on a new Push Arena.** First content unit. Motion is what the text cannot show. Include the wall, the wind icon, and three trials so the listed misconceptions lose.
4. **Light-into-the-eye path plus four materials in a beam.** One asset serves 1-A now and 4.3 later. Stops the eye-beam idea before it sits for three years.
5. **Fair Test Lab, copied from Mystery Machine, with Dry Note and one Guard the Sand.** Grade 2 needs it. Every later barrier unit reuses Guard the Sand with a new question, so do not build Shoreline Defender, a flood game, and a dune game.
6. **5.3 two-rail matter/energy board.** Grade 5 is later in the year, but this is the worst content error and it is a diagram, not a new engine. Build it before any food-web game. Scene & Web can follow.
7. **4.1 heat-arrow-and-coat SVG, then the collision bar on Push Arena.** The coat picture is an hour of drawing and prevents two hard misconceptions. The collision bar should wait until Push Arena exists so it is not a one-off.
8. **Short Circuit skin: polarity, insulator gap, circulating arrows, hot-short stop.** Needed before any 4.2 page goes into classrooms. The safety stop matters as much as the PE.

Not in the first eight, on purpose: Notice Walk arcade, Particle Dance, a Gravity Duel reskin, a reaction-time game, a second erosion sim, and any seasons-tilt animation.
