 Living Things and Their Homes
 What do living things need, and how do homes and helpers support them?
 Plants and animals need certain things, and their homes give them what they need. You will find out what living things need to survive, how habitats supply food, water, shelter and space, how living things change the places they live, and how people can help.

 Concept 1
Plants and animals have needs
 Plants and animals need things such as water, air, food, sunlight, and space to survive.
 [IMG: Photo of a brown deer drinking at a forest stream surrounded by ferns and trees.] A deer needs water, food, air and space. A forest stream gives it water.
 Analogy
 Think of a needs list, like a shopping list. Everyone has a list of things they must get to stay alive. A plant’s list: water, sunlight, air. An animal’s list: food, water, air. Both need space to live.
The lists are not the same, but each living thing needs everything on its own list.
“Plants need water and sunlight. Animals need food, water, and air.”
 Try it
 Sort living things and nonliving things; then match needs cards.
“Does it need water?”

 Concept 2
Homes meet needs
 A habitat provides food, water, shelter, and space; different habitats have different living things.
 [IMG: Photo of a sand dune on Lake Michigan with beach grass and a gull flying over the blue lake.] A sand dune is a different habitat. Beach grass and gulls live here, not the same living things as a forest.
 Diagram
 Pond, forest, and sand dune.
“A pond gives fish water.”
 [SVG IMAGE] A pond gives fish water. A forest gives deer shelter and food. A dune has sand and grass for plants and birds that live there.Text version of this picture

 Try it
 Sort Michigan animals into habitats.
“Where does the frog live?”
Sort Michigan animals and plants into habitats.
 Try it
 Guess first: What does your animal need in its home? Plan it first.
What you need: clay or play dough, paper, crayons, blue paper for water, a small tray.
Do: Pick one animal, such as a frog, deer, or fish. Make its home on the tray. Add blue paper for water, paper trees or grass for shelter, and little clay bits for food. Put the animal in.
Look for: Check the model against the needs list: does it have water, food, shelter, and space?
What it shows: A habitat is the place that meets an animal’s needs. If one need is missing, the home does not work.
“My model has water, shelter, and food.”

 Concept 4
People can reduce harm
 People can make choices that help local land, water, air, and living things.
 Analogy
 We can be habitat helpers. Your two hands are like helper hands for a place. Picking up litter helps animals stay safe. Planting a native flower gives bees food.
Each small action is one helping hand for the land, water, air, or living things.
“We can be habitat helpers.”
 Try it
 Guess first: How many pieces of litter will we find in the schoolyard? Guess a number.
What you need: an adult, gloves, a bag, a camera or paper to draw on. Or: a native flower plant, a small pot or garden spot, a watering can.
Do: Take a “before” photo or drawing of the spot. Then, with an adult, pick up the litter into the bag and count the pieces (do not touch sharp things). Or plant the native flower and water it. Take an “after” photo or drawing.
Look for: Compare before and after. Was there less litter? How many pieces did we pick up? What helped?
What it shows: Real actions change a real place, and the pictures are our evidence.
Safety: Always wear gloves, never touch sharp objects, and wash hands after.
“We pick up litter. We take a before photo and an after photo.”
 Data
 Before/after photo or tally chart.
“Before: 10 litter pieces. After: 0.”

 Try it

 Tutor notes: misconceptions, standards, pacing
 Pacing: 5 sessions of 30 minutes.
 Common misconceptions to listen for:
“Plants eat soil.” Correct: plants need water, light, and air; soil holds the plant in place and gives it water and minerals.
“Plants need only dirt.” Correct: plants also need water, sunlight, air, and space.
“A habitat is only where an animal sleeps.” Correct: a habitat provides food, water, shelter, and space.
“Living things do not change their environment.” Correct: organisms can change their surroundings.
“Only people change the environment.” Correct: animals and plants can change environments too.
“People cannot help nature.” Correct: local actions can reduce harm.
“Litter does not harm habitats.” Correct: litter can harm land, water, and living things.
 Limits and cautions:
K-ESS3-3 is only partial unless students complete one real local action with evidence.
Litter picks need adult supervision, gloves, and permission. Do not touch sharp objects.
If planting is not possible, use a photo dataset and a classroom “before/after” simulation, but note that real action gives the strongest evidence.
This unit does not cover external parts, biomimicry, parent-offspring behavior, or young resembling parents; those belong in 1-B and 2-B.
Food needs are simplified; ecosystems are more complex and are developed later in 5.3.
