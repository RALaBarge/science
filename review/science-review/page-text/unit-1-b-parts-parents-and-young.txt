 Parts, Parents, and Young
 How do outside parts, parents, and young help living things survive?
 Living things have parts that do jobs, and young animals and plants grow up to look like their parents, but not exactly. In this unit you will connect beaks, feet, roots and leaves to what they help a living thing do, see how people copy nature's designs, and look at how some animal parents care for their young.

 Concept 2
Biomimicry design
 We can copy the shape or job of a plant or animal part to solve a human problem.
 [IMG: Close photo of a spiky burr stuck to a gray wool sock, with its tiny hooks caught in the knit.] A burr has tiny hooks that grab fabric. People copied that idea to make hook-and-loop fasteners.
 Analogy
 A burr is like a sticky strap (hook-and-loop fastener). Burr’s tiny hooks = the hooks on the strap. Fur or a sock = the loops it grabs. People copied the burr’s hooks to make the strap.
“A burr grabs. We can copy it.”
 Try it
 Guess first: Which part of a plant or animal could help you solve a problem? Pick one.
What you need: picture cards, paper, tape, fabric scraps, cotton balls, paper clips, pipe cleaners, a small tray.
Do: Choose a problem: stay dry, carry seeds, or stay warm. Pick a part to copy: a leaf (stays dry), fur (stays warm), or a burr (grabs). Build a model helper. Test it: sprinkle water, hold it on your hand, or try to pick up paper “seeds”.
Look for: Did the helper work? What part of nature did it copy? What would you change?
What it shows: People get ideas from nature’s parts. This is called biomimicry.
Safety: Small pieces need an adult close by. Do not use live wild animals.
“I copied a part from nature. I tested it.”

 Concept 3
Parent-offspring behavior
 Some animal parents feed, protect, or guide their young. We look at examples and ask what the parent does and how it helps the young survive; not every animal has this care.
 [IMG: Photo of a mother duck walking at a pond edge followed by four ducklings in a line.] Some animal parents guide their young. The ducklings follow their mother.
 Analogy
 Parent care is like a helper blanket. The blanket = what the parent does (feeds, guards, guides). The one under the blanket = the baby animal. A blanket keeps you warm and safe, and care keeps young animals safe.
Where it breaks: not every animal parent cares for its young.
“Care keeps young safe.”
 Try it
 Read-aloud text, short video, or picture cards: robin feeding chicks, ducklings following mother, deer hiding fawns.

 Hands-on · Design or investigate
Try it at home or in class
 Biomimicry Helper Challenge. Problem: a person needs help staying dry, carrying small seeds, or keeping warm. Students choose a natural external part to mimic, such as a leaf, fur, shell, or burr. They build a model helper, test it, and record whether it worked. Criteria: the helper works in a simple test and the student can name the natural part copied. Constraints: use only kit materials. This earns 1-LS1-1 when built and tested.
 Try itWhat you need
Michigan plant and animal picture cards, paper, tape, fabric scraps, cotton balls, paper clips, pipe cleaners, crayons, magnifier, small tray. Optional: bean seeds and cups if comparing plant young and adults.
Safety: Small parts need supervision. If using soil, seeds, or outdoor plants, wash hands after the activity. Do not use live wild animals.
 Try it
 What you do
 1. Parts and jobs (10 minutes). Play the match game above. Make a class chart: part and job.
 2. Biomimicry helper (25 minutes). Pick a problem and a natural part, build a helper, test it, and record whether it worked.
 3. Parent care (10 minutes). Listen to a read-aloud or look at picture cards. Fill in: parent, action, how it helps the young.
 4. Alike and different (10 minutes). Look at adult and young pairs. List what is alike and what is different.

 Tutor notes: misconceptions, standards, pacing
 Pacing: 5 sessions of 30 minutes.
 Common misconceptions to listen for:
“Baby animals are exactly like their parents.” Correct: young are very much like, but not exactly like, their parents.
“Young animals do not need parents.” Correct: many parents feed, protect, and teach young; care helps survival.
“Only body parts help; behaviors do not help.” Correct: behaviors such as feeding and protecting also help survival.
“Biomimicry means copying a whole animal exactly.” Correct: we copy a useful shape, pattern, or job.
“Living things cannot help solve human problems.” Correct: plant and animal parts can inspire useful designs.
“External parts are only for looking nice.” Correct: external parts have jobs.
“Young are not like parents at all.” Correct: young often resemble parents in many observable ways.
 Limits and cautions:
Parent care varies across animals; teach these as patterns, not rules for every species.
Avoid saying all baby animals are cute or helpless; some are independent quickly.
Biomimicry models must stay simple; a good test matters more than a fancy build.
This unit does not teach cells, genes, or inherited trait mechanisms. Those belong in later grades.
Simplification: “parents help young” is a grade-1 pattern; real animal behavior is more varied.
