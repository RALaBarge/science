 Energy, Speed, and Collisions
 How can we use speed, collisions, heat, light, and sound as evidence that energy moves?
 Energy can be moved from place to place, and we can find evidence of it. You will connect speed to energy, watch what collisions do, follow heat as it moves from warmer to cooler things, and see how sound and light can carry energy too.

Speed and energy
 Faster moving objects can cause bigger changes, which is evidence that they have more energy.

 Concept 2
Collisions change energy
 When objects collide, motion energy can move, so things can speed up, slow down, or change shape.
Drop a ball and watch where its energy goes. Pick a ball. These are made-up numbers to show the idea, not measurements.
Pick a ball and see that its energy of motion becomes a little heat, sound and bending. The total stays the same.
 Try it
 Marble target
a marble rolling into a small block target.
“Roll the marble. Watch the target move. Energy moved.”
Predict first: If the marble rolls faster, will the block move farther or less far?
You need: a marble, a small block or box, a ruler, a ramp or book.
Set a ramp. Place the block at the bottom.
Roll the marble from a low start. Measure how far the block slides.
Roll from a higher start. Measure again.
Do each three times.
What you should see: The higher start makes the marble faster, so the block moves farther. Your numbers will differ.
Why it matters: Energy of motion moves from the marble to the block.

 Concept 3
Heat moves from warmer to cooler
 Heat energy transfers from warmer objects to cooler objects until temperatures become more equal.
 [SVG IMAGE] Heat always moves from warmer to cooler. A coat does not add cold or heat: it slows the heat leaving your body.Text version of this diagram
Top: a warm cup and a spoon, with a heat arrow along the spoon from the warm cup toward the cooler end. Bottom: two rows with a warm body on the left and cold air on the right. With no coat the heat arrow is thick and heat leaves fast. With a coat in between, the heat arrow is thin, so heat leaves slowly. A coat does not make heat; it slows the heat leaving.
 [SVG IMAGE] A warm object cooled from 40 C to 37 C to 35 C over three readings, as heat moved to the cooler air around it.Text version of this graph
Line graph of temperature in degrees C over three readings: 40, 37 and 35. The line slopes down.
 Analogy
 Hot cocoa and spoon
a spoon in a cup of warm cocoa.
“The spoon gets warm. Heat moved from cocoa to spoon.”
 Try it
 Cup warming
hands wrapped around a warm cup. Adult handles warm water.
“Your hands warm up because heat moves into them.”
Predict first: Will your hands feel warm? Where does the warmth come from?
You need: a mug with warm (not hot) water; an adult pours it.
Feel the empty mug first.
Have an adult fill it with warm water.
Wrap both hands around it for one minute.
Notice: Your hands feel warm and the water cools a little. Heat moved from the warm water, through the mug, into your hands.
Why it matters: Heat always moves from the warmer thing to the cooler thing.

 Concept 4
Sound and light can move energy
 Sound vibrations and light can also transfer energy to other things.
 Try it
 Sound moves rice
rice on plastic wrap over a cup or near a speaker; the rice jumps when sound is made.
“Make a sound. Watch rice jump. Sound moved energy.”
Predict first: Will rice jump when you make a loud sound near the wrap?
You need: a bowl, plastic wrap, a rubber band, a pinch of rice, a pot and spoon.
Stretch plastic wrap tight over the bowl. Put the rubber band on.
Sprinkle rice on top.
Hold a pot near the bowl and bang it with a spoon.
Notice: The rice dances. Sound is moving air, and it shakes the plastic and the rice.
Why it matters: Sound carries energy from one place to another.
 Try it
 Light warms a hand
a hand held near a lamp, not touching it. Adult handles the warm lamp.
“Hold your hand near the lamp. It feels warm. Light moved energy.”
Predict first: Will your hand feel warm near a lamp, without touching it?
You need: a desk lamp with a regular bulb; an adult switches it on.
Hold your palm 20 cm below the lamp for 10 seconds.
Move it away and compare.
Never touch the bulb; it gets hot.
Notice: Your hand warms up in the light. Light moved energy to your hand.
Why it matters: This is how the Sun warms the Earth.

 Hands-on · Design or investigate
Try it at home or in class
 Fair-test task: “Which collision makes the target move farthest?” Students choose one variable to change, such as marble speed or marble mass. They keep the target the same, predict outcomes, test three times, graph results, and write one evidence sentence.

 [SVG IMAGE] The target moved 2, 2 and 3 cm from the low ramp, and 7, 8 and 8 cm from the high ramp. Same marble, same target: only the ramp height changed.Text version of this graph

 Try itWhat you need
toy car or marble, cardboard ramp, books, small block or target, paper cups, spoon, warm water, thermometer if available, flashlight or lamp, plastic wrap, rubber band, small speaker or drum, rice, paper, pencil, tape.
Safety: adult handles warm water and warm lamps; do not stare into bright lights; keep water away from Chromebooks.
 Try itWhat you do
Test ramp height and block movement. Run marble collisions and predict outcomes. Observe heat transfer with a warm cup. Observe sound moving rice and light warming a hand. Record real observations.
Let a toy car roll down a low ramp, then a high one. Measure how far it pushes a block each time (15 minutes).
Roll a marble into a block. Predict first, then test three times (15 minutes).
Warm your hands on a warm cup. Bang a pot near rice. Hold a hand under a lamp (15 minutes).
Write one evidence sentence, such as: "The higher ramp made the car faster, and the block moved farther."
Notice: Faster things carry more energy. Energy can move from one thing to another.
Why it matters: Energy is easier to track when you measure what it moves.

 Tutor notes: misconceptions, standards, pacing
 Pacing: five 30-minute sessions.
 Common misconceptions to listen for:
“Faster objects have less energy.” Correct: faster objects usually have more energy.
“Energy disappears in a collision.” Correct: energy can transfer and change motion.
“Cold flows into things.” Correct: heat transfers from warmer to cooler.
“A coat makes heat.” Correct: a coat slows heat leaving your body.
“Sound cannot move things.” Correct: sound vibrations can move small objects.
“Still things have no energy.” Correct: energy can be present or transferring even when the evidence is subtle.
 Limits and cautions:
4-PS3-2 is only partly earned here; electric current is intentionally deferred to 4.2.
Grade 4 does not require kinetic-energy formulas; the unit uses speed evidence only.
Sound-moving-rice can be subtle; the provided dataset guarantees evidence if equipment fails.
