Land and Water, Fast and Slow

How does land change fast and slow, and how can we model land and water?

You did this before / what is new. New in grade 2: you test wind and water on sand with repeated trials, and you compare erosion-guard solutions with data and build a Michigan land/water model.

Concept 1

Earth events can be quick or slow

Some changes to land happen fast, like floods and volcanoes; others happen slowly, like erosion and canyon carving.

Analogy

Fast like a door slamming; slow like a crayon wearing down.

“Some changes are quick. Some are slow.”

Diagram

Photo pairs: flood before/after, volcano, canyon, dune.

“Fast: flood moved soil. Slow: water carved rock.”

Try it

Sort picture cards or video stills into fast or slow. Add a resource token: water, soil, sand, or rock.

“I see a volcano. It is fast.”

Data

Class evidence chart: event, clue, fast/slow, Earth material.

“Earthquake: fast. Dune moves: slow.”

Concept 2

Slowing wind and water

Wind and moving water can move soil and sand; plants, rocks, and barriers can help.

Try it

Fan air gently over dry sand or soil with a folded card; pour a little water. Add grass, sticks, or stones and test again.

“Wind moved sand. Grass helped stop it.”

Data

Before/after drawing or finger-width movement chart.

“No plants: sand moved. With plants: sand stayed.”

Real world

Beach grass helps hold dunes.

“Beach grass holds sand.”

Concept 3

Model your local land and water

A model can show the shapes and kinds of land and water near us.

Diagram

Simple Michigan-like map or photo with lake, river, hill, and dune.

“Land can be hills. Water can be lakes.”

Try it

Build a clay or paper landform-water model of a Michigan-like area. Add blue paper for water.

“Make a lake. Make a dune.”

Data

Model feature checklist.

“Our model has a lake and a river.”

Concept 4

Where water is; solid or liquid

Water is found in lakes, rivers, oceans, ice, snow, clouds, and underground; it can be liquid or solid.

Diagram

Simple Michigan map with Great Lakes, rivers, snow, and underground water.

“Water is in lakes, rivers, snow, and ice.”

Try it

Sort pictures: solid water vs. liquid water.

“Snow: solid. Lake: liquid.”

Data

Class chart: Where do we find water?

“We found water in 5 places.”

Real world

Earth resources include water, soil, trees, rocks, and sand.

“Farms use soil and water.”

Hands-on · Design or investigate

Try it at home or in class

Erosion Guard Test. Problem: wind or water moves sand in the tray. Students sketch and build two guards, such as grass, stones, or a paper barrier. They test each guard 3 times with the same blow or pour, measure sand movement, and choose the better solution using evidence.

Try it

What you need

tray, sand or soil, straw, small cup or dropper, grass clippings/twigs/stones/paper barriers, clay or paper, blue paper, picture cards or printed photos, simple Michigan map, crayons, paper.

Safety: Wash hands after sand/soil trays. Do not blow on sand. Use a folded card to fan it and keep sand away from faces and eyes. No coin cells or magnets are required.

Try it

What you do

Sort fast/slow Earth-change sources, test erosion guards, build a Michigan-like land/water model, and sort solid/liquid water pictures.

Words to know

land
the solid part of Earth’s surface.
water
liquid found in lakes, rivers, and oceans. Frozen water is ice or snow.
fast
happens in a short time.
slow
happens over a long time.
erosion
moving soil, sand, or rock by wind or water.
barrier
something that blocks or slows movement.
model
a smaller or simpler way to show something.
map
a drawing that shows places.
solid
keeps its shape, like ice.
liquid
flows, like water.
Tutor notes: misconceptions, standards, pacing

Pacing: 5 sessions of 30 minutes.

Common misconceptions to listen for:

  • “Land never changes.” Correct: land changes fast and slow.
  • “Only earthquakes and volcanoes change land.” Correct: wind, water, and ice also change land, often slowly.
  • “Wind is just clouds moving.” Correct: wind is moving air.
  • “Water is only in lakes.” Correct: water is also in rivers, ice, snow, clouds, and underground.
  • “A model is the same as the real place.” Correct: a model is smaller and simpler, but it helps show ideas.

Limits and cautions:

  • Sand/soil trays require handwashing and gentle blowing rules.
  • Quick events like floods or earthquakes should be taught calmly; focus on evidence and preparation, not fear.
  • Models are simplified; they are not the real place.
  • If an extension uses lights or magnets, use ready-made lamps only and keep magnets away from Chromebooks.

Michigan Science Standards this unit earns:

  • 2-ESS1-1: Use information from several sources to provide evidence that Earth events can occur quickly or slowly.
  • 2-ESS2-1: Compare multiple solutions designed to slow or prevent wind or water from changing the shape of the land.
  • 2-ESS2-2: Develop a model representing the shapes and kinds of water and land in a local environment.
  • 2-ESS2-3: Obtain information to identify where water is found on Earth and that it can be solid or liquid.
