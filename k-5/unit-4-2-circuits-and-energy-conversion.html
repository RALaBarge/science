Circuits and Energy Conversion

How can we make a safe circuit that changes electrical energy into useful light?

On a snowy Michigan night, the power can go out. Flashlights and battery lights keep a room lit. A working circuit is a complete loop. Building one shows how electrical energy changes into light, sound, or heat.

Concept 1

A circuit is a complete loop

Current is the flow of electric charge. It moves only around a complete loop. A gap anywhere stops current in the whole loop, even on the side with no gap. A coin cell is a small, flat, round battery, like a watch battery. Its two sides are marked + (positive) and - (negative). A terminal is the metal spot where a wire touches the battery or LED.

Analogy

Train track

a looped track with a small train.

“If the track is broken, the train stops. If the wire path breaks, the current stops.”

Diagram

Open vs closed

When the loop has a gap, the path is open. Current cannot flow, so the bulb is off. A loop with no gap is closed, and current flows around it.

“Open path: no light. Closed path: light.”

Data

LED direction log

An LED has two legs. The long leg is the + (positive) side. The short leg is the - (negative) side. An LED lights only when its long leg is on the battery’s + side.

Concept 2

Conductors and insulators

Some materials let current flow easily. These are conductors, like metal. Others block current. These are insulators, like plastic and dry wood. Electric wires use both: metal inside, plastic outside.

Real world

Safe wiring

A conductor lets current flow through it. An insulator blocks current.

Metal is a conductor. The plastic coat is an insulator, so the current stays inside the wire.

“Metal carries current. Plastic coating keeps it safe.”

Concept 3

Switches open and close circuits

A switch is a gate in the loop. Closing it joins the wires and lets current flow. Opening it breaks the path. That is why a flashlight has a button. Open means the gate is apart and the path is broken. Closed means the gate touches and the path is complete.

Diagram

Switch open and closed

Here the switch is closed. The metal strip touches both screws, so the path is complete and current can flow. Lifting the strip away opens the switch and breaks the path.

“Open switch: path broken. Closed switch: path complete.”

Real world

Flashlight

A flashlight has a switch inside.

Pressing the button closes a gap in the loop. Then the current can flow from the battery to the bulb and back.

Concept 4

Circuits convert energy

Energy changes form. A battery stores chemical energy. The circuit carries it as electrical energy, and devices turn it into light, sound, or heat.

Diagram

Energy conversion

The battery gives electrical energy from its + side and its - side. The LED turns part of it into light and part into heat.

+-

The battery gives electrical energy from its + and - terminals. The LED turns part of it into light and part into heat.

“The battery gives electrical energy. The LED makes light.”

Diagram

A light-up card

A card circuit puts a coin cell and an LED in one loop with copper tape. A paper clip can be added to make a switch.

+-

Current flows out of the + side of the coin cell, through the LED and the switch, and back to the - side. Close the switch and the LED lights.

Real world

Where the rest goes

A circuit never turns all of its electrical energy into light or sound. Some of it turns into heat in the wires, the LED and the battery. That heat is wasted energy. It warms the air around the circuit instead of making light. The less energy is wasted as heat, the more of it does the job.

On a snowy Michigan night, an old-style porch light bulb can get too hot to touch. Most of its electrical energy becomes heat, and only a little becomes light.

Real world

Doorbell

A doorbell is a switch and a bell in one loop.

Pressing the button closes the loop. Electrical energy shakes the bell, and the bell makes sound.

“Electrical energy becomes sound.”

Tutor notes: misconceptions, pacing

Pacing: five 30-minute sessions.

Common misconceptions to listen for:

  • “Electricity is used up in the bulb.” Correct: energy transfers; current flows in a loop.
  • “One wire is enough.” Correct: a complete path is needed.
  • “The battery stores current like water.” Correct: batteries store chemical energy; current is moving charge.
  • “All circuits are dangerous.” Correct: small battery circuits are safe, but wall electricity is dangerous.
  • “LEDs work either direction.” Correct: LED lights only one way; flip it if it is dark.
  • “Plastic and wood conduct electricity like metal.” Correct: metals conduct; plastic and dry wood block almost all current.
  • “An open switch still lets current flow.” Correct: an open switch breaks the path.

Limits and cautions:

  • Coin cells are safe for this grade, but they are a swallowing hazard for younger siblings; tape them when stored.
  • LEDs can stay dark if reversed or if the battery is weak; check orientation and battery freshness.
  • The unit does not teach voltage, series/parallel circuits, or current measurement.

Before you play

What to try in the game

  • Build a closed loop. The bulb lights.
  • Open the loop by moving one wire. The light goes out.
  • Add a switch. Closed turns the light on; open turns it off.
  • An LED lights only when its long leg (+) is on the battery’s + side. If it is dark, flip it.
  • Metal lets current through. Plastic and dry wood block almost all of it.
