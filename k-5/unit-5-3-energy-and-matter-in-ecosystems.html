Energy and Matter in Ecosystems

Where does the matter and energy in living things come from, and how does it move?

Concept 1

Plants build matter from air and water

Plants get most of their growth material from air and water; sunlight gives energy, but sunlight is not matter.

Diagram

Plant inputs

water and air (matter) go into the plant; sunlight (energy, not matter) shines on the leaf; the plant builds new plant material and gives off oxygen.

“Plants use water and air, which are matter, and light, which is energy, to make food.”

Data

Tree and soil argument

table: young tree 2 kg, soil 100 kg; older tree 50 kg, soil 99.9 kg.

“The soil barely changed. The tree got big. Most mass came from air and water.”

Try it

two cups with beans, one in light, one in dark.

“Keep water the same. Change only light.”

Concept 2

Food energy came from the Sun

The energy in your lunch was captured by plants from sunlight; your body uses it for warmth and motion.

Analogy

Food as stored sunlight

apple and sandwich with a small Sun icon behind them.

“Food is stored Sun energy.”

Diagram

Energy chain

Sun → corn → chicken → child.

“The Sun gives energy to plants. Animals eat plants. You eat food. Your body uses energy to move and stay warm.”

Try it

Trace three foods

cards for apple, chicken, and corn.

“Draw arrows back to the Sun for each food.”

Real world

Michigan apple

Michigan apple tree and apples.

“An apple tree captured sunlight. The apple has food energy.”

Concept 3

Matter cycles through ecosystems

Matter moves from plants to animals to decomposers and back to soil/air; it does not disappear.

Diagram

Food web

plant → rabbit → fox; dead matter → decomposers → soil/air.

“An arrow points to the eater. Food matter moves along it and can be reused. Energy moves along it too, but it ends up as heat and motion.”

Try it

String web

students holding cards and string.

“If one part changes, the web changes.”

Data

Decomposer dataset

table: dead leaves 100 g at start; after decomposer time, leaf pieces 35 g, richer soil 55 g, and about 10 g gone into the air as gas.

“Dead leaves break down. Matter returns to soil and air.”

Real world

Lake Michigan web

algae, small fish, larger fish, birds, decomposers.

“Great Lakes food webs include many organisms.”

Hands-on · Design or investigate

Try it at home or in class

Fair test: Does light affect bean sprout growth? Change only light; keep water, container, and seed type the same. Measure and graph sprout height, and note color and stem strength. Dark-grown beans often stretch tall but stay pale and weak. Use the plant data to discuss why light matters for energy, while using the tree/soil data to argue where plant matter comes from.

Try it

What you need

bean seeds, clear cups, paper towels or soil, water, magnifier, pictures of habitats, string, index cards, tape, pencil.

Safety: wash hands after touching soil or beans; do not eat classroom beans or soil; clean up water spills.

Tutor notes: misconceptions, standards, pacing

Pacing: five 30-minute sessions.

Common misconceptions to listen for:

  • “Plants eat soil.” Correct: plants use water, air, and light; soil provides minerals and support.
  • “Plants get most of their mass from soil.” Correct: most plant mass comes from air and water.
  • “Sunlight becomes the plant’s matter.” Correct: sunlight is energy; matter comes from air and water.
  • “Food energy comes from the package, store, or refrigerator.” Correct: food energy originally came from the Sun through plants.
  • “Dead matter disappears.” Correct: decomposers break it down and matter returns to soil/air.
  • “Food webs only show who eats whom.” Correct: arrows point to the eater, so they also show how food matter and energy move; matter is reused, energy ends up as heat and motion.
  • “Food webs show where animals sleep.” Correct: food webs show feeding relationships.
  • “Decay should be stopped.” Correct: decay recycles matter.

Limits and cautions:

  • Cells are not taught here; full cell work belongs in later middle-school units.
  • Photosynthesis is introductory: inputs and outputs only, no chemistry.
  • The tree/soil argument simplifies soil minerals; they matter, but they are not the main source of mass.
  • Decomposers releasing gases is simplified as “matter returns to soil/air.”
  • Provided datasets guarantee the argument if scales, soil, or growing time are unavailable.

  • 5-PS3-1: Use models to show that energy in food was once energy from the Sun.
  • 5-LS2-1: Model movement of matter among plants, animals, decomposers, and environment.
